Keychain UI — design v0

Wireframes at the real 368 × 448 panel ratio. Amber on black is a placeholder identity; the pixel font is a placeholder too (final: a hand-drawn dot-matrix font). Nothing here is built yet.

1 · The idea in one paragraph

It is an object you carry, not a watch you read. There is no home screen, no app grid, no swipe-down shade. The crown is a rotary mode dial, like the mode dial on a camera. You sit in Carry (ambient art that looks good hanging off a belt), turn to pick a mode, press to enter it. Everything that is not a mode (notifications, calls) interrupts briefly and goes away.

2 · Controls — one switch, one crown, one touch panel

The crown is the button (press = push the crown in). So the whole input vocabulary is small enough to learn in a minute:

InputIn CarryOn the DialInside a mode
Rotatenext / previous scenemove between modes (haptic click per notch)the mode's main dial: volume, scroll, timer, tempo…
Pressopen the Dialenter the modethe mode's main action: play/pause, fire shutter, flip coin…
Double-press—back to Carryback to the Dial (the universal “get me out”)
Long pressscreen off (and the same press wakes it) — works everywhere
Press + rotate——second action: seek in Music, brightness elsewhere
Touch: swipe left/rightoff by default (brushing trousers does nothing)—page through a deck: QR cards, clock faces
Touch: tap / dragofftap a mode name to entertap targets, drag lists

Honest cost of double-press: a single press has to wait about 0.2 s to be sure no second press follows. Tapping the big on-screen button is instant, so the camera shutter and torch use touch for the instant case.

Things you hadn't listed — my additions

  • Name-badge Me card as the first card, and as a Carry scene — at events it is the thing people actually read.
  • Sign / marquee: scrolling dot-matrix text (“ASK ME ABOUT THIS”). Very eye-catching on a belt, trivial to draw.
  • Pet as the system mascot: it looks up on a notification, bobs with music, types while Claude works, sleeps when idle. One character ties every mode together — good reel material.
  • Counter / countdown: days since you started, days until a trip or birthday.
  • Incoming call: shows the caller; press answers, hold declines (audio stays on the phone / AirPods).
  • Haptic signature per mode and per app: you can tell WhatsApp from Mail with your hip.
  • Demo loop: a mode made for your reel — one tap and it walks through everything.

Left out on purpose: weather and calendar (need data from the phone, which we cannot get without an app), Wi-Fi QR (you said no), 2FA codes (you said no).

5 · Faces, pets and QR cards without reflashing — “packs”

Yes, do it the way the stock app did for watch faces, and make it better. The keychain holds a few active slots; your phone or laptop holds an unlimited library; you swap what lives in the slots over Bluetooth. Two kinds of pack, both pure data (so a bad pack can never brick the device):

Pack typeWhat is in itWhy it is cheap
Assetsphotos (16-colour dithered), pixel-art sprite sheets for the pet, QR codes, marquee textstored in spare flash, not in the firmware
Recipesclock faces and ambient art written as a short list of shapes (rings, arcs, hands, text) tied to the timeour renderer already draws from a list of shapes, so a face is a few hundred bytes — “tons of clock options” is nearly free
Honest unknowns
  • Free flash for assets — I have not measured it. A full-screen photo is 330 KB uncompressed (16-bit), ~80 KB at 16 colours. Expect a handful of photos or many pixel sprites; a retro pet is tiny (4× scaled 2-bit sprites).
  • Uploading from the iPhone needs a web page in Bluefy (Safari has no Bluetooth). Laptop (Chrome on Arch / Mac) works directly. A tiny native app via Xcode on the Mac is possible later but not needed.
